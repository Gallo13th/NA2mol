# Assessment of the RNA2smol ligand-leakage audit

Yes—the audit substantially addresses the concern about direct ligand memorization, but it does not fully establish that the ECFP4 recovery result is independent of previously encountered chemistry. The strongest evidence is the low exact-match rate among generated molecules and the persistence of RNA2smol's recovery advantage when reference actives with supervised-training matches or close analogues are excluded. The main remaining issues are the influence of ligand-only pretraining chemistry and a discrepancy between the audit's reconstructed recovery statistics and the manuscript's published values.

I reviewed the summary statistics, recovery sensitivity analysis, and the relevant code included in the uploaded file. My assessment is below.

These results are encouraging. Most unique molecules generated by RNA2smol are not exact standardized copies of supervised-training ligands, and only a relatively small fraction have ECFP4 similarity of at least 0.5 to that training set.

This does not prove that the model has never memorized a chemical pattern. Generative models can learn recurring fragments and molecular motifs without reproducing entire training molecules. But it does argue against the simple explanation that RNA2smol's output consists primarily of copied training ligands.

The comparison with other methods is useful too. The exact-match fraction is similarly low for the evaluated baseline generators, and their rates of close similarity vary. RNA2smol does not stand out as the method that most frequently reproduces fine-tuning chemistry.;





My judgment: This part of the audit substantially reduces concern about direct supervised-training memorization of generated outputs.

## 2. Reference-active overlap exists, but its effect on recovery has been tested

The other important question is not just what the generator produces, but whether known benchmark actives used to calculate ECFP4 recovery already occur in the supervised-training data.

The audit identifies the following overlap:

| Reference benchmark | Exact match to fine-tuning training ligands | Maximum ECFP4-TS ≥ 0.5 |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| SMRT-Net            | 18.0% of 50 unique actives                  | 58.0%                  |
| DUD-E               | 2.16% of 35,237 unique actives              | 36.38%                 |

For SMRT-Net, 9 of 50 reference actives have exact training matches, and 11 of 27 targets have at least one associated active with an exact training match. For DUD-E, the overall exact-match fraction is much smaller, although 99 of 101 targets have at least one exact-match active because each target is associated with many active ligands.;



The distinction between ligand-level and target-level overlap is crucial. The DUD-E result does not mean that 98% of its active molecules are duplicated; it means that nearly every target has at least one active among the much larger active-reference collection that overlaps a training ligand.

The audit then performs the more informative test: it recalculates target-level recovery after excluding reference actives with exact or close matches to the supervised-training set.

These findings support two useful conclusions.

First, removing exact training-active matches does not eliminate RNA2smol's recovery advantage. For DUD-E, the observed recovery changes only slightly, from 0.695 to 0.685, with all 83 evaluated targets retained. For SMRT-Net, the value increases after filtering; however, the target set changes from 27 to 24, so the increase cannot be attributed to filtering alone.

Second, removing reference actives with ECFP4 similarity of at least 0.5 to supervised-training ligands does not eliminate RNA2smol's advantage over the external generators on the resulting common eligible-target sets. On SMRT-Net, RNA2smol's 0.249 exceeds the corresponding results for MolCRAFT, Pocket2Mol and ED2Mol; DUD-E shows a similar ordering at 0.580.;







This is meaningful evidence against the hypothesis that the advantage in the recovery analysis is driven solely by exact duplication or close supervised-training analogues.

There is one important qualification: comparisons between different rows use different reference sets, and the eligible SMRT-Net target population changes across filters. These results support the model's comparative performance on each filtered evaluation set, but the changes between filter rows are not clean estimates of the causal effect of removing overlap.

## 3. The unresolved issue: overlap with the ligand-only pretraining library

This is where the audit needs a more nuanced interpretation.

The pretraining library contains 2,287,199 unique canonical SMILES. Among the reference actives, 72% of SMRT-Net active ligands and 90% of DUD-E active ligands have a pretraining-library neighbour with ECFP4-TS ≥ 0.5. Exact pretraining matches are much less common: 4% for SMRT-Net and 9.19% for DUD-E.;







That is substantial chemical familiarity, but it is not equivalent to supervised target leakage. The pretraining library contains ligand structures without pocket labels; a close analogue in that library does not mean the model was shown the test compound bound to the test target.

The generated-output audit is consistent with that distinction. For RNA2smol, approximately 16.8% of the sampled SMRT-Net outputs and 26.5% of the sampled DUD-E outputs have an ECFP4 neighbour at or above 0.5 in the pretraining library. These values are based on a stratified sample of generated outputs rather than the complete generated-molecule collection. On SMRT-Net, the corresponding fractions for Pocket2Mol and ED2Mol are also around 25–27%; RNA2smol is not unusually close to pretraining chemistry compared with those models.;





This is reassuring: the main model does not appear to produce a uniquely high fraction of near-duplicates of its pretraining library.

### What about the pretraining-filtered recovery?

Here the results are more complicated.

| Benchmark          | Original recovery | After excluding active references with a pretraining neighbour ≥ 0.5 | Eligible targets |
| ------------------ | ----------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------- |
| SMRT-Net, RNA2smol | 0.218             | 0.052                                                                | 27 → 14          |
| DUD-E, RNA2smol    | 0.695             | 0.240                                                                | 83 → 80          |

The corresponding filtered values also fall for the external baselines. On SMRT-Net, RNA2smol no longer leads every baseline on this filtered set; ED2Mol reaches 0.085 compared with RNA2smol's 0.052. On DUD-E, RNA2smol remains ahead of the baselines under the same filtered reference definition.;





I would not interpret this as proof of pretraining leakage. Excluding reference actives with a close neighbour in a 2.29-million-molecule library changes which chemical structures are represented in the recovery metric. It selects for less common chemistry, and on SMRT-Net it removes enough target coverage that only 14 targets remain eligible.

Nevertheless, this result does establish something important: the ECFP4 recovery metric is sensitive to the chemical space represented by the reference actives and the pretraining library. It cannot be presented as a measure of novel chemistry wholly independent of the model's learned ligand prior.

### One additional calculation is needed

For each filtered analysis, compare the filtered recovery against the original recovery recalculated on exactly the same target subset.

For example, in the pretraining \\(T\geq0.5\\) sensitivity analysis, compute the original \\(A\_{\mathrm{obs}}\\) on the same 14 SMRT-Net targets that remain eligible after filtering. Then compute the filtered \\(A\_{\mathrm{obs}}\\) on those same 14 targets. This separates the change in reference chemistry from the change in target composition.

Apply the same principle to the exact-match and fine-tuning-analogue filters.

The current analysis keeps the same eligible target set across methods within each filter, which makes each within-filter comparison meaningful. But because the target set changes between filters, the difference between the original and filtered values cannot be attributed entirely to overlap removal.

This is a focused reanalysis, not a demand for a new model or new experiment.

### What to do

Before reporting the filtered recovery analysis as a validation of the published result:

1. Identify precisely which target IDs, reference ligands and generated molecules were used for the original Fig. 2 ECFP4 calculation.
2. Reproduce the manuscript's original \\(A\_{\mathrm{obs}}\\) values using those same inputs and the published threshold range.
3. Only then apply the overlap filters to that same evaluation framework.

This is not a reason to discard the leakage audit. The overlap tables are independently informative. It is a reason to reconcile the sensitivity-analysis baseline before drawing a final conclusion about the robustness of the headline recovery result.

## 5. Overall verdict and recommended next steps

# Verdict: Mostly addressed, with two remaining checks

Direct memorization of supervised-training molecules: substantially addressed

Exact matches make up only 0.08–0.16% of RNA2smol's generated unique molecules. The active-reference filtering analysis also preserves RNA2smol's lead over the external baselines on the resulting common target sets.

Reference-active overlap with supervised training: addressed provisionally

There is measurable overlap, particularly for SMRT-Net, but excluding exact matches and close training analogues does not eliminate RNA2smol's comparative recovery advantage in the audit's filtered analyses. The changing target subsets mean this conclusion should be confirmed with fixed-target sensitivity comparisons.

Ligand-only pretraining overlap: characterized, but not eliminated as an influence on ECFP4 recovery

The pretraining library contains considerable chemically similar material. This is not equivalent to target-specific leakage, but the filtered recovery results show that the metric is sensitive to which chemical references are retained.

Reconstruction of the original recovery result: unresolved

The audit's DUD-E recovery statistics differ from the published values, and the audit uses 83 targets rather than the manuscript's reported 101 shared systems. Reconcile the evaluation inputs before using the sensitivity analysis as conclusive validation.

### Suggested manuscript paragraph

After resolving the reconstruction discrepancy, I would add a short paragraph in the Results section or as an extended-data analysis. The following draft reflects the present audit findings but should only be finalized after the target-set reconciliation.

**Sensitivity of active recovery to training-set chemical overlap.** We quantified chemical overlap between the evaluation reference actives and both the supervised fine-tuning datasets and ligand-only pretraining library using exact molecular identity, Bemis–Murcko scaffold overlap and ECFP4-Tanimoto similarity. Exact matches to the fine-tuning training set affected 18% of SMRT-Net reference actives and 2.2% of DUD-E reference actives, while close analogues with ECFP4-Tanimoto similarity ≥0.5 were more common. Nevertheless, exact-match exclusion and the exclusion of close supervised-training analogues preserved RNA2smol's comparative target-recovery advantage on the corresponding common eligible-target sets. Exact reproduction of fine-tuning training molecules among generated RNA2smol outputs was rare (0.16% for SMRT-Net and 0.08% for DUD-E).

Chemical similarity to the ligand-only pretraining library was more extensive: 72% of SMRT-Net and 90% of DUD-E reference actives had a pretraining neighbour with ECFP4-Tanimoto similarity ≥0.5. Because this library contains ligand structures without target-pocket labels, such similarity does not itself establish target-specific information leakage. However, excluding reference actives with close pretraining analogues substantially altered recovery estimates, indicating that retrospective ECFP4 recovery remains sensitive to the chemical composition of the reference set. These analyses therefore support the robustness of RNA2smol's recovery advantage to exact and close supervised-training overlap, while distinguishing that finding from the influence of prior chemical knowledge acquired during ligand-only pretraining.

### My final recommendation

I would not launch a new model-training campaign at this stage. Your audit already provides substantial evidence against simple supervised-ligand memorization as the explanation for RNA2smol's performance.

The next work should be limited to two targeted tasks: reproduce the original ECFP4 recovery results on precisely the same evaluation population, and recalculate original-versus-filtered recovery on fixed target subsets. Then you can report what the overlap audit establishes, acknowledge the influence of ligand-only pretraining without calling it target leakage, and retain ECFP4-TS as a legitimate retrospective chemical-recovery metric.

That would give the manuscript a much stronger and more defensible answer to the leakage concern without turning it into an unnecessarily broad new benchmarking project.
